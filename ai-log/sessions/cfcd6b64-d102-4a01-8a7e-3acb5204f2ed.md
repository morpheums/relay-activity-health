## Design, section 3 of 5: Statistics rules

For each site, and separately for the account total:

1. **Baseline weeks:** the 8 complete local weeks just before the selected week, **zero-filled**. A week only counts if it falls within the data's coverage and on or after the site's first activity. Weeks before the data begins aren't treated as zero activity.
2. **Fewer than 4 usable weeks → `insufficient_data`.** This covers early weeks, new sites, and account 20.
3. **Typical** = the median of the baseline weeks.
4. **Spread** = the larger of:
   - 1.4826 × MAD (median absolute deviation, scaled to be comparable to a standard deviation), and
   - √median (the natural noise of counts; this stops a band of zero width when a site is very steady or very quiet).
5. **Normal range** = typical ± 2 × spread, with the lower end clamped at 0. That's what the admin sees: "74 this week, usually 24–121."
6. **Status:** `below` or `above` when the count is outside the range, otherwise `normal`.
7. **Deviation** = (count − typical) / spread. Locations are sorted by |deviation| descending, then by name, with `insufficient_data` last.

- A median plus MAD is robust to the spike. Account 6's baseline for Jul 20 includes the 880 week: the **mean is 171**, but the **median is 72.5** and the range is 24–121, so the actual 87 correctly reads as **normal**.
- It's all basic descriptive statistics: nothing fitted, nothing forecast, so it stays inside the "no ML" line.

**The honest trade-off:** about 5% chance flags means a 15-site account will typically see **about one flagged site in a normal week**. ## 1. The statistics rule needs to change

Simulated against every site-week with a full baseline (spike week excluded). "Drop to 0" means we replace that week with zero at a site that usually has 3 or more. "Doubled" means we replace it with twice the median.

| Rule | False flags ↑ / ↓ (all activity) | Catches drop to 0: all · calls · leads | Catches doubling (all) | Displayed range consistent with the flag? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Current** (median ± 2·max(MAD-spread, √median)) | 3.8% / 1.0% | 78% · 37% · **0%** | 78% | Yes |
| Current + "zero when usually ≥ 3 → below" | 3.8% / 1.1% | 100% · 100% · 100% | 78% | **No**: "0, usually 0–8, ▼ Below" contradicts itself |
| **Square-root transform** (Anscombe) + robust z | **1.4% / 2.9%** | **98% · 95% · 96%** | 48% | Yes |

**My recommendation: the square-root transform.** It's the standard way to put counts on a scale where noise is the same size at every volume, so the same "±2" means the same probability whether a site does 4 or 80 a week. It's still descriptive statistics, not ML:
- `T(x) = 2·√(x + 3/8)`. Median and MAD are computed on `T(baseline)`, spread = max(1.4826·MAD, 1), and the site is flagged when `|T(count) − T-median| > 2·spread`.
- The range is converted back to counts, so it's **asymmetric**, the way count ranges really are. A median of 4 becomes "usually 1–9", so a week of 0 is visibly outside it.
- It flags drops more readily than rises, which is the right bias for "needs attention". The overall false-flag rate drops from about 5% to about 4%. The spike is still caught at 15/15 sites.

**The cost:** it's less sensitive to moderate rises at small sites. A site going from 3 to 6 isn't flagged, and statistically it shouldn't be (that happens by chance about 8% of the time).

**A limit no rule can fix:** at these volumes, halving at a single site is undetectable (0–6% under every rule). Only large moves show up per site, and the account row catches smaller ones. That goes in the README as a stated limitation, not hidden. The golden values in §7 will be recomputed under the new rule.
