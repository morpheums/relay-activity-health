# Small Sample Problems with c-Chart Control Limits

## The Core Problem

Traditional 3-sigma control limit equations for c-charts assume the Poisson distribution is symmetrical. However, when defect counts are small, this assumption breaks down. The document explains: **"If c is small, the Poisson distribution is not symmetrical and the equations are no longer valid."**

Specifically, the guidance states: **"If cbar < 2, the control limit equations are not valid."**

## Why This Matters

The standard equations produce inaccurate control limits that don't properly reflect the actual probability of false alarms. Visual demonstrations in the article show how the Poisson distribution shifts from highly skewed (at low averages) to symmetrical (at higher averages).

## Recommended Alternative

Rather than standard equations, practitioners should derive limits directly from Poisson probabilities. The article provides a lookup table for cbar values from 0.02 to 2.48, establishing control limits where **"the probability of obtaining a point beyond the control limit is less than or equal to 0.003"**—maintaining the intended 3-per-1000 false alarm rate despite the distribution's asymmetry.

## Minimum Count Rule of Thumb

The critical threshold is stated plainly: maintain **cbar ≥ 2** to use standard equations reliably. Below this, consult Poisson-based tables.

**Synthesis**
- **The standard is an expected value plus a band.** A value outside the band is marked above or below, with how far. What sits underneath varies: ML for hourly or daily data, simple robust statistics for weekly data (Adobe weekly, Datadog basic, Grafana, Tableau, control charts). At our weekly grain, simple statistics are the norm, not a compromise [INF].
- **Call-tracking and multi-location tools show only % change and peer benchmarks.** A per-location "normal for you" range would set us apart. At about 5 a week, % change is very noisy: 5 → 3 reads as −40% [INF].
- **Small counts.** None of the surveyed products documents a count-specific (Poisson) band. The control-chart literature does, and warns that normal approximations break at low counts [DOC].
- **My simulation (8 normal weeks, then test week 9)** [SIM]:

| Rule for "outside usual" | Share of normal weeks flagged, per location |
|---|---|
| Outside 8-week lowest–highest | 14–17% |
| Outside roughly 10th–90th percentile | 30–36% |
| Median ± 3 robust spreads | 3–7% |
| Count-based 99% range around the median | about 1% |

  With 10 locations at a 15% false-flag rate, about 80% of Mondays show at least one false "needs attention" [INF].

**Recommendations for our design**
1. **Keep:** per-location median of the last 8 complete weeks, "usually X–Y", the four statuses, and ranking. All are supported by the docs above.
2. **Band:** use the count-based 1st–99th percentile range around the median, or median ± 3 × max(robust spread, √median, 1). Show whole numbers, never below 0 [SIM/DOC].
3. **Not enough history:** require at least 6 of the 8 weeks. Treat a median under 2 a week as "too few to judge", and show no band rather than a misleading one [DOC/INF].
4. **Ranking:** rank by how unlikely last week's count is, not raw distance, which over-ranks busy locations. Label each location with the count and % difference [INF].
5. **Wording:** plain words, neutral "within usual range", no σ or "anomaly". Add a note: "Based on your last 8 full weeks" [DOC].
6. **Optional:** a "well outside" tier or a two-weeks-in-a-row rule, and a way to exclude closure weeks later [DOC/INF].
